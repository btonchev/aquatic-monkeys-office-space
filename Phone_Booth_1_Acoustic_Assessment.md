# Phone Booth 1 -- Acoustic Performance Assessment

## 1. Test setup

An indicative acoustic test was conducted to assess how much speech
noise from Phone Booth 1 reaches the two surrounding workstations.

Measurements were taken using: - iPhone at Desk 1 - iPad at Desk 2 -
NIOSH Sound Level Meter on both devices - Approximately 3 minutes of
continuous speech playback per test - Devices positioned at seated head
height (approximately 1.2 m) - [AcoustiAngel](https://freesound.org/people/AcoustiAngel/sounds/739037/)

The same speech recording was played from a laptop inside the booth. The
door remained closed during the standard test.

A background-noise measurement was taken first, followed by a test with
the door closed and a second test with the door perimeter temporarily
sealed using tape.

## 2. Measurement results

| Test condition | Desk 1 – LAeq | Desk 2 – LAeq |
|---|---:|---:|
| Background noise (T0) | 27.1 dB(A) | 27.0 dB(A) |
| Speech inside booth, door closed (T1) | 35.2 dB(A) | 31.7 dB(A) |
| Speech inside booth, door perimeter taped (T1.1) | 36.6 dB(A) | 33.5 dB(A) |

### Increase above background noise

| Test condition | Desk 1 | Desk 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| T1 – Door closed | +8.1 dB | +4.7 dB |
| T1.1 – Door taped | +9.5 dB | +6.5 dB |

## 3. Interpretation

### Sound transmission to the workspace

Speech inside Phone Booth 1 produces a measurable increase in sound
levels at both workstations. The effect is more pronounced at Desk 1,
where the increase above background noise reaches 8.1 dB.

This indicates that the booth does not fully contain speech noise under
the tested conditions.

### Effect of sealing the door

Temporarily sealing the door perimeter with tape did not result in a
measurable improvement. The recorded LAeq values were actually higher
during this test.

This does not prove that the tape made the acoustic performance worse.
Differences in playback level, source position, environmental conditions
or measurement variability may explain the change.

### Potential sound transmission paths

Further investigation should consider: - Door seals and perimeter gaps -
The upper interface between the booth and surrounding ceiling -
Ventilation openings - Glass panels and their joints - Structural
connections that may transmit sound

These are potential transmission paths, not confirmed defects.

## 4. Preliminary conclusion

Phone Booth 1 currently does not demonstrate convincing sound isolation
for a quiet coworking environment.

Speech noise is measurable at both desks, with Desk 1 experiencing the
greater impact. The temporary door-sealing test did not demonstrate an
improvement.
